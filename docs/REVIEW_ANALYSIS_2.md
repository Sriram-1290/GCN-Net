# One-Page Review-Paper Analysis

**Project:** GBP-Net: A Graph Band-Power Network with Cross-Subject Pre-Training for EEG Motor Imagery Decoding  
**Investigators:** Saligram Sriram Shravan, Parcha Seetha Ramarao, M Priyansh · **Batch No.:** 22 · **Guide:** Dr. Gagan Kumar  
**Institution:** Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Neil Gogte Institute of Technology (Affiliated to Osmania University)

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| **Title of published review paper** | Graph Neural Network-Based EEG Classification: A Survey |
| **Authors** | Dominik Klepl, Min Wu, Fei He |
| **Journal / conference** | *IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering*, vol. 32, pp. 493–503, 2024. DOI: 10.1109/TNSRE.2024.3355750 |
| **Publication year** | 2024 |
| **Main review topic** | Graph neural network (GNN) architectures for electroencephalography (EEG) decoding across emotion recognition, epilepsy, motor imagery, sleep staging, and neurological disorders, structured modularly by GNN pipeline components. |
| **Number of papers reviewed** | 63 peer-reviewed journal articles and preprints. |
| **Databases used for searching** | Google Scholar and ScienceDirect (queried on 1 November 2022). |
| **Search keywords** | `("Graph neural network" OR "Graph convolutional network") AND ("Electroencephalography" OR "EEG")`, screened via title and abstract. |
| **Classification of reviewed studies** | Six modular categories following the GNN computation pipeline: (1) brain-graph definition (structural/distance vs. functional; predefined vs. learnable); (2) node features (time-domain, frequency-domain, or differential entropy); (3) node-feature preprocessing; (4) graph-convolution operator (spatial, spectral ChebConv, or spatio-temporal); (5) node pooling (Graclus, DiffPool, SAGPool); (6) readout and graph embedding formation. |
| **Important comparison tables** | Six thematic comparative tables: Table I (graph construction), Table II (node features), Table III (feature preprocessing), Table IV (convolution layers), Table V (node pooling), and Table VI (readout embeddings). Figure 3 illustrates task distributions (emotion: 41%, epilepsy: 25%, motor imagery: 19%), but without task-disaggregated performance benchmarking. |
| **Main findings** | (1) Chebyshev spectral graph convolution (ChebConv) is the predominant operator across the literature, routinely adopted without comparative justification against spatial layers. (2) Raw timepoints and differential entropy dominate node features. (3) Functional correlation graphs capture long-range coupling but are noise-sensitive, whereas distance graphs are sparse and static. (4) Architectural innovation is concentrated in graph construction rather than convolution operators. |
| **Research gaps identified** | (1) Rigidity across montages: fixed Chebyshev filters fail to transfer to datasets with variable electrode counts. (2) Acute absence of transfer-learning and cross-subject pre-training experiments. (3) Sub-band frequency and cross-frequency interactions remain under-exploited. (4) Total absence of controlled ablation studies comparing GNN layers against unconstrained spatial baselines. |
| **Future research directions** | Controlled empirical benchmarks comparing spatial vs. spectral vs. attention graph layers; large-scale cross-dataset pre-training; dynamic frequency-aware graph topologies; and graph pooling operators that accommodate variable electrode geometries. |
| **Reference style used** | Numbered IEEE citation format in order of appearance in the published survey. |
| **How this paper is useful for our project** | **(1) Grounding our Baseline:** It directly reviews GCNs-Net (Hou et al., 2024), confirming that ChebConv is the field's de facto default. This directly motivated our modular ablation isolating ChebConv against unconstrained spatial operators on identical splits. **(2) Validating Pre-Training:** Its finding of an acute "lack of transfer learning experiments" justified GBP-Net's Stage 1 cross-subject pre-training across 92 subjects to defeat per-subject data scarcity. **(3) Structuring our Classification:** Its pipeline taxonomy informs our four structural axes. **(4) The Evaluation Integrity Gap:** Klepl et al. analyzed architectures but remained blind to *data leakage* (timepoint shuffling, transductive graphs, eval-mode batch norm). Our review bridges this gap via code-level auditing of `SuperBruceJia/EEG-DL` and PRISMA 2020 protocol controls. |
| **Main limitations of this review** | (1) **Task Conflation:** Fails to disaggregate results by paradigm, ignoring the specific biophysics of motor imagery (ERD/ERS band-power phenomena) and the class–run structural confound in EEGMMIDB. (2) **Protocol Blindness:** Evaluates reported accuracies at face value without auditing validation split granularity, normalization leakage, or sample definitions. (3) **Inverted Ordering Unaddressed:** Does not examine whether computing node features prior to graph convolution destroys phase cancellation. |
| **Why this review was selected** | **Direct Relevance:** It is the only published survey reviewing the exact GNN method family of our approved Major Project title and specifically analyzes our baseline (GCNs-Net). **Verifiability:** Gold open access with complete full-text availability. **Synthesis Role:** Comparing Klepl et al. against motor-imagery surveys (Saibene et al., 2024), transfer reviews (Wu et al., 2022), and leakage audits (Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023) enabled our Review-Paper-2 to synthesize 36 peer-reviewed works (27 PW-1 core survey papers + 9 methodological standards) into an audited blueprint for GBP-Net. |
